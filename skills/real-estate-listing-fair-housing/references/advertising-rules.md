# Licensee Advertising & Disclosure Reference

This is a **different body of law from Fair Housing.** Fair Housing is anti-discrimination law with broadly consistent principles across jurisdictions. What follows is licensee advertising regulation — set by each state real estate commission or provincial regulator, and it varies substantially. A listing can be perfectly clean on Fair Housing and still violate it.

**The screening rule:** you cannot know every state's rules and must not pretend to. Flag the *category*, name what is typically required, and tell the agent to confirm with their broker. Never state a specific state's requirement as fact unless it appears below.

---

## 1. Universal categories — check on every listing

These apply in some form nearly everywhere. Flag them regardless of jurisdiction.

**Brokerage identification.** Most states and provinces require the employing brokerage's name in advertising. A listing description with only the agent's name is a common violation. If the copy names the agent but no brokerage, flag it.

**Owner/agent disclosure.** If the licensee has any ownership interest in the property, that must generally be disclosed in the advertising itself.

**Truthfulness and material facts.** No false or misleading statements. This is where invented details become a legal problem rather than an editing problem — see section 5.

**Advertising another broker's listing.** Generally requires permission. If an agent asks for copy about a property that isn't theirs, ask.

**Equal Housing Opportunity.** Many brokerages require the logo or wording on advertising. Not the skill's job to insert, but worth noting to the agent.

## 2. Arizona — ADRE (detailed)

Arizona enforces advertising rules actively and this is one of the stricter states.

- **A.A.C. R4-28-502(E) and (K)** — the licensee must identify the employing broker's legal name or DBA "in a clear and prominent manner." For online and social content, current guidance is that this must be visible **without the reader needing to scroll**.
- **A.A.C. R4-28-502(L) and A.R.S. § 32-2163(D)** — online advertising, web content, and SMS marketing targeting Arizona residents are subject to the same rules as print. There is no "it's just social" exemption.
- **Owner/agent** — a licensee advertising property they own or co-own, including through an entity or trust, must place "Owner/Agent" in the advertisement.
- **No implication of a private sale** — a licensee may not advertise property in a way that implies no agent is involved.
- **Rule revisions effective December 13, 2025** expanded the advertising rule to explicitly cover online, electronic, **and AI-assisted marketing**, and increased designated-broker responsibility for advertising produced by the brokerage.

**What this means for output:** AI-assisted listing copy is squarely within the rule. If the agent is in Arizona, the brokerage identification requirement is not optional and their designated broker carries responsibility for what they publish. Say so.

The most frequently cited Arizona violations are failure to identify the employing broker clearly and prominently — in listings, in social posts, in team advertising, and on branch signage.

## 2a. California — DRE

The distinguishing requirement: **the licence number.** Under B&P Code § 10140.6 and Regulation § 2773, a licensee must disclose their eight-digit licence identification number and the responsible broker's name on all solicitation materials intended to be the **first point of contact** with consumers — business cards, stationery, and websites the licensee owns or controls. The full eight digits are required including leading zeros, and in written material the type size must be no smaller than the smallest type used in the piece.

Not every listing is first-point-of-contact material, and "for sale" or open-house signage with no licensee branding is exempt. But agent-branded property marketing usually is. If a California agent is producing anything with their own name on it, flag the licence number.

Team names must comply with B&P § 10159.5 — must include "team" or "group" and must not suggest a separate brokerage.

## 2b. Texas — TREC

TREC Rule 535.155 requires every advertisement to include the name of the licence holder or team placing it, **plus the broker's name**. The distinguishing rule is a size test: the broker's name must be **at least half the size** of the largest contact information for any sales agent, associated broker, or team name in the ad.

Also enforced:

- An ad may not imply that a sales agent is responsible for the operation of a brokerage. A sales agent may not use "owner" or "president" titles in advertising.
- Team names must end in "team" or "group," must not contain "brokerage," "company," or "associates," and must be registered with TREC by the broker before use.
- Under Rule 535.154, advertising another broker's exclusively listed property without permission or without naming the listing broker is deceptive, as is failing to promptly remove closed or terminated listings.
- For social media and texts, the required information may sit on a linked profile page as long as it is reachable by a direct link.
- TREC generally allows a 14-day window to correct a violation before enforcement.

## 2c. Florida — FREC

Rule 61J2-10.025 requires the **licensed name of the brokerage firm** — as registered with DBPR — in all real estate advertisements, and that advertising be done so a reasonable person knows they are dealing with a licensee. Nothing fraudulent, false, deceptive, or misleading.

Two Florida-specific points:

- When the licensee's personal name appears, at minimum their **last name as registered** must be used, and the personal name must be **no larger than** the registered brokerage name.
- For internet and electronic advertising, the brokerage name must be placed **adjacent to, immediately above, or immediately below the point of contact information** — any address, email, phone, or fax by which someone would contact the agent or firm. This is not a "one-click" rule.

Team names (Rule 61J2-10.026) may not include agency, associates, brokerage, brokers, company, corporation, properties, property, real estate, realty, or similar words, and may not be printed larger than the registered brokerage name.

## 2d. Nevada — NRED

All Nevada real estate advertising must include the brokerage firm name as registered with NRED. The agent's name alone is not sufficient. Advertising conditions and disclosure requirements sit under NRS 645.315.

## 2e. North Carolina — NCREC

Rule 21 NCAC 58A .0105 prohibits **blind ads**: an advertisement may not be confined to contact information alone, and every ad must indicate it is the advertisement of a broker or firm. The firm name must be disclosed.

NCREC does **not** recognise a one-click rule — the firm name should be immediately visible rather than a click away. Provisional brokers must also identify their supervising broker, and the Broker-in-Charge is responsible for advertising by affiliated brokers, including social media.

## 2f. Georgia — GREC

Rule 520-1-.09 is unusually broad and unusually strict. It's the only rule in this file that regulates advertising and discrimination in the same provision.

**Firm name and telephone number.** When advertising a specific property, the advertisement must include a firm name registered with the Commission **and a telephone number for the firm**. Georgia is the only jurisdiction in this file requiring a phone number as well as a name.

**Size, prominence, and frequency.** The licensed firm's name must appear in **equal or greater size, prominence, and frequency** than the name of any affiliated licensee or group of licensees. Not half the size, as in Texas — equal or greater.

**Internet advertising.** Advertising on the internet must disclose the firm name and telephone number **on every viewable web page**, not once on the site.

**Advertising must be in the firm's name.** All advertising by associate brokers, salespersons, and community association managers must be under the direct supervision of their broker and in the name of the firm.

**Written permission required.** A licensee may not advertise any property without first securing the owner's written permission. When advertising a property listed with another firm, the licensee must clearly and conspicuously disclose that fact and name the listing firm, unless that firm has waived it in writing.

**Discriminatory advertising — 520-1-.09(4).** This is the provision worth surfacing to any Georgia agent:

> A licensee shall not advertise real estate when the advertisement is directed at or referred to persons of a particular race, colour, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin. **The contents of any advertisement must be confined to information relative to the real estate itself.**

That second sentence is a state licensing requirement that says, in plain terms, what this skill's core rule says: describe the property, not the people. In Georgia, "describe the property, never the buyer" is not just best practice for avoiding a Fair Housing complaint — it is the text of the licensing rule, enforceable against the agent's licence independently of any federal claim.

When a Georgia agent's copy trips a Fair Housing flag, say so: the same phrase is a licence exposure under GREC rules, not only a federal Fair Housing risk.

**Misleading advertising** that is inaccurate in any material fact, or that misrepresents property, terms, values, services, or policies, is prohibited. If a licensee becomes aware their own client is advertising inconsistently with the rule, they must take steps to stop it.

## 3. Ontario — RECO / TRESA

Trevor's home market and the TRREB audience.

Ontario real estate advertising is regulated by RECO under the Trust in Real Estate Services Act and its Code of Ethics. Core requirements to flag:

- The **brokerage's registered name** must appear in advertising, and must be at least as prominent as the salesperson's name or team name.
- A salesperson may not advertise in a way that suggests they are operating independently of their brokerage.
- Advertising must not be false, misleading, or deceptive.
- Team and personal-brand names are permitted but carry additional identification requirements.

Do not state specific RECO wording requirements as fact — direct the agent to their brokerage's advertising policy and RECO's current advertising guidelines, which are updated periodically.

## 4. MLS rules (separate again, and commonly breached)

MLS rules are contractual, not statutory, but they get agents fined faster than anything else here. Typical public-remarks restrictions:

- **No agent contact information** in public remarks — no phone, email, website, or showing instructions. This is the single most common MLS remarks violation.
- **No commission or compensation references** in public remarks.
- **No open-house or showing instructions** in public remarks (these belong in private remarks).
- **Character limits** vary by MLS.
- **No links** to external sites.

When writing MLS remarks, never include contact details, compensation, or showing instructions even if the agent supplies them. Move them out and tell the agent they belong in private remarks or the appropriate field.

**Compensation note:** following the NAR settlement changes, offers of buyer-broker compensation are handled outside MLS public fields in most markets. Never write compensation terms into listing copy. If an agent asks, flag it and refer them to their broker.

## 5. Truthfulness and material facts

The skill's no-fabrication rule exists for this reason, not just for accuracy.

- **Never invent** square footage, lot size, room counts, year built, school names, distances, or system ages. An invented number in published advertising is a misrepresentation exposure for the agent.
- **Measurements** — if the agent supplies square footage, use it as given. Don't convert, round, or estimate.
- **Puffery vs. misrepresentation** — "stunning kitchen" is opinion and safe. "Newly renovated kitchen" is a factual claim and needs to be true. Flag factual-sounding claims the agent hasn't substantiated.
- **Unverifiable superlatives** — "best value in the neighbourhood," "lowest price per square foot" are factual claims dressed as marketing. Flag them.
- **"Safe neighbourhood," "low crime"** — flagged under Fair Housing as proxy language *and* here as an unsubstantiated claim. Two reasons, same fix.

## 6. How to screen for this

Run this pass alongside the Fair Housing screen, and report it as a **separate section** so the agent can see which flags are discrimination risk and which are licensing/advertising risk. They go to different people — Fair Housing to the broker and possibly counsel, advertising rules usually to the broker's compliance policy.

Order of operations:

1. Ask the jurisdiction if it isn't known and any flag depends on it.
2. Screen for the universal categories in section 1 — always.
3. If the state or province appears above, apply those specifics.
4. If it doesn't, name the category and say plainly that the specific requirement varies and their broker or state commission is the source of truth. **Do not guess at a state's rule.**
5. Strip contact info, compensation, and showing instructions from MLS remarks automatically, and say why.

## 7. Quick comparison — the distinguishing requirement per state

| Jurisdiction | The thing most often missed |
|---|---|
| Arizona (ADRE) | Employing broker named clear and prominent, no scrolling; owner/agent disclosure; AI-assisted marketing explicitly covered since Dec 13, 2025 |
| California (DRE) | Eight-digit licence number on first-point-of-contact materials, plus responsible broker's name |
| Texas (TREC) | Broker's name at least half the size of the largest agent/team contact info |
| Florida (FREC) | Registered brokerage name adjacent to/above/below the contact info; agent's name no larger than the brokerage's |
| Georgia (GREC) | Firm name **and phone number**, equal or greater size/prominence/frequency than the agent's; every web page; content must be confined to the real estate itself |
| Nevada (NRED) | Brokerage name as registered — agent name alone is not enough |
| North Carolina (NCREC) | No blind ads; firm name immediately visible, no one-click rule |
| Ontario (RECO) | Brokerage registered name, at least as prominent as the salesperson or team name |

## 8. What this reference does not cover

Only the jurisdictions in the table above are detailed. Every other state and province has its own commission rules, and several have requirements not listed — team-name restrictions, licence-number disclosure, font-size and prominence standards, and rules on the use of "REALTOR®."

Notably **not covered:** South Carolina, Tennessee, New Mexico, Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana, Oklahoma, Utah, Colorado, and all others.

When an agent is outside the covered jurisdictions, say plainly that this is a category to check rather than a ruling, and name the general requirement (a brokerage is almost always required to be identified) without asserting that state's specific standard.

Rules change. These were verified in August 2026 — the Arizona revisions took effect December 13, 2025 and Texas had changes effective January 1, 2026. Treat anything older than a year as needing re-verification.
